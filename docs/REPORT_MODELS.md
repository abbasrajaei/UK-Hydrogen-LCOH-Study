# Three report examples

These examples were reviewed to choose a suitable structure. They guide the presentation. NESO remains the source of the main numerical inputs.

| Example | Relevant parts | What we use |
|---|---|---|
| [DOE: Hydrogen Production Cost from PEM Electrolysis](https://www.hydrogen.energy.gov/docs/hydrogenprogramlibraries/pdfs/19009_h2_production_cost_pem_electrolysis_2019.pdf) | Analytical basis, input table, cost breakdown, single-input sensitivity and two-input contours. See printed pages 2–10. | The main reporting model. It is closest to our cost-driver and equipment-performance study. |
| [UK government: Hydrogen Production Costs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hydrogen-production-costs-2021) | Cost method, technical assumptions, levelised results and sensitivity tests. See Sections 2, 5, 6 and 7. | Clear cost boundaries and a table of common assumptions. Its annex also supports our separate calculation check. |
| [IRENA: Green Hydrogen Cost Reduction](https://www.irena.org/publications/2020/Dec/Green-hydrogen-cost-reduction) | Figure 1 links investment, power price and operating hours. Section 2.4 discusses equipment design trade-offs. | Explain the engineering reason behind each cost result. Keep the wider policy discussion outside our study. |

## Chosen structure

The DOE example is the closest match. It starts with an analytical basis and input assumptions. It then presents the cost breakdown and sensitivity results. Its contour plots show how pairs of inputs affect hydrogen cost.

Our report follows that sequence: aim, data, methodology, starting cost, input sensitivity, combined factors and conclusions. We add the equipment-price saving needed to offset lower efficiency.

The examples have wider scopes than our model. We use their reporting structure. We do not copy their numerical results or add analyses for which we have no data.
